# Red-Team Critique: NFL DFS Greenfield Engine Blueprint

**Date:** 2026-08-28 · **Subject:** `plan.md` (repo root, verified identical to the reviewed upload) · **Season clock:** NFL kickoff is September 10, 2026, thirteen days out.

**Verdict in one line:** This is unusually literate DFS engineering with three load-bearing defects: the game-day evidence chain names sources that are dead or unusable under the plan's own terms policy, the portfolio objective demotes expected value to a tiebreak, and the scope cannot ship before the season it is built to play.

---

## 1. Executive Synthesis

The proposed engine is a local, Windows-native, fully FOSS pipeline that treats DraftKings as a file boundary. Operator-downloaded salary and entry CSVs are frozen and hashed; public NFL data (nflverse releases, official injury pages, NWS weather, market lines) is snapshotted as immutable evidence with pass/fail gates; hierarchical regularized models turn that evidence into opportunity and efficiency distributions; a possession-level joint game simulator produces three sealed scenario banks (DESIGN to generate candidates, SELECT to choose the portfolio, REFEREE to independently verify it); MILP solves over DESIGN draws create a diverse candidate bank; a cold-start ownership model generates complete legal opponent fields; portfolio selection picks the Nash-product point on a two-axis Pareto frontier, Upside = P(at least one top-1% finish) versus NonWashout = P(gross ≥ fees), assigned to exact Entry IDs; a three-pass QA loop with a repair whitelist and an independent byte-level certifier produce the manual upload package; post-contest standings feed an automatic weekly refit with champion/challenger deployment and rollback. Everything runs from one PowerShell launcher against SQLite and Parquet, with an Excel workbook as the operator surface.

Credit where due, plainly: several choices are ahead of most hobbyist stacks and some commercial ones. Candidates come from scenario optima rather than jittered mean projections. The ownership model is required to satisfy roster-level accounting, and multiplying player ownerships into a "lineup probability" is explicitly banned. The sealed REFEREE bank that selection and repair may never inspect is the correct defense against optimizing to simulation noise. Byte-hash certification with a strictly manual DraftKings boundary is the right legal and operational posture. The APPG metamorphic test, the honest refusal to pretend route participation is freely available, and the `NOT_YET_DUE` inactive semantics with a mandatory late-swap path are all correct calls. On metric feasibility generally: every signal the modeling section names computes deterministically from free nflverse/PFR/NGS data (WOPR from target and air-yard shares, PROE from the PBP `xpass` field, CPOE natively in PBP, pace from play timestamps, snap shares from PFR, xFP benchmarkable against ffopportunity), with in-season route participation the lone exception, which the plan already handles correctly by proxy.

The defects concentrate in four places: what free data actually exists on game day, what the objective function actually optimizes, compute and memory budgets that were never computed, and scope measured against a calendar that starts in thirteen days.

## 2. Fatal Blockers & Critical Flaws

**B1. The game-day evidence chain is broken at both of its hard gates.** The source table's injury row falls back to "nflverse injury data," which does not exist: the nflverse maintainers state the injuries source died after the 2024 season, with no current data and no ETA ([nflverse data schedule](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/nflverse_data_schedule.html)). The primary sources for both hard-gate rows (official NFL injury pages, NFL.com/inactives) sit behind NFL.com terms that prohibit systematic retrieval without written consent ([NFL terms](https://www.nfl.com/legal/terms/)), which the plan's own source policy ("HTTP clients only for sources whose terms permit collection") then forbids fetching. nflverse depth charts update once daily at 7AM UTC (3AM ET in season), useless for a 90-minute-to-lock decision, and the feed's schema changed after 2024 (timestamped appends, no week field), so any adapter written from older documentation breaks on contact. Net effect: the T-110 inactives gate, the single most catastrophic-loss-preventing control in the design, has no automated path at all. It is operator-manual by construction, and the plan miscasts that as a "fallback." Fix in D1.

**B2. The portfolio objective demotes EV to a tiebreak, which is a money-losing objective in GPPs.** The frontier axes are P(top-1%) and P(break-even); expected net payout only breaks statistical ties. Top 1% of a 150,000-entry field is 1,500th place, and of a 238,000-entry field is 2,380th: low-single-digit multiples of entry fee, while DraftKings' flagship GPP routes roughly a fifth of the entire prize pool to first place and the bulk of EV to the top ~0.01–0.1%. Maximizing P(top-1%) therefore optimizes for the min-cash-adjacent region, and maximizing P(break-even) alongside it drags the portfolio further toward floor plays. The Nash product of two floor-flavored probabilities is exactly the muddled compromise between opposed objectives this plan claims to avoid, and it will systematically forfeit the correlated ceiling builds that justify entering GPPs at all. Worse, the actual supplied fixture is two $5 entries, for which P(gross ≥ $10) is dominated by whether one entry roughly doubles: a nearly degenerate axis that points at chalk cash builds. A portfolio can sit on this frontier and be plainly negative-EV; nothing in the selection step would notice. Fix in D2.

**B3. The frontier stands on the least trustworthy component in the system.** Both axes are probabilities computed against generated opponent fields and payout thresholds, i.e. against the cold-start ownership model the plan itself grades `COLD_START_FIELD_MODEL` and refuses to call calibrated. Honest labeling does not help if selection consumes the uncalibrated field at full strength: ownership estimation error propagates into false leverage, phantom duplication edges, and wrong payout thresholds. The plan evaluates low/base/high ownership-reaction states but never says how selection uses them, so the architecture's most important defensive decision is unspecified. Fix in D3.

**B4. No compute or memory budget exists anywhere in the plan, and the naive implementations do not fit.** Concrete arithmetic the plan should have contained: a 150k-entry field scored across 10k scenarios is a 5.6 GiB float32 matrix (8.9 GiB at Milly Maker size), which cannot be materialized on a consumer machine alongside everything else; a 50k-candidate bank against 10k scenarios is another 1.9 GiB; the Classic SELECT bank alone is ~16.8M simulated plays, which is fine vectorized across scenarios (< 1–2 min) but 8–28+ minutes per bank as scalar Python, repeated across DESIGN/SELECT/REFEREE and again at every T-3h refresh, QA repair cycle, and late-swap reforecast. SciPy's HiGHS wrapper (`scipy.optimize.milp`) has no warm starts and no solution pools, the wrong interface for thousands of sequential scenario solves. None of the phase gates carries a runtime number or a hardware baseline. On the current text, the first live Sunday discovers whether the T-30 window is feasible. Fix in D4.

**B5. The plan cannot be built before the season starts and defines no interim capability.** Kickoff is September 10. The six phases with their acceptance matrices (possession simulator with accounting invariants, full-field generation with seven calibration targets, conditional late-swap recourse, champion/challenger registry with automatic rollback, property and metamorphic test suites) are a multi-month solo build under professional conditions, longer through copy-paste operation. Nothing usable exists until Phase 5 completes: earlier phases certify CSV echoes and projections but cannot enter a slate. There is no defined degraded mode, so the realistic outcomes are either missing weeks 1–8 of the season or abandoning the governance mid-build under deadline pressure, which defeats its purpose. Fix in D5.

**B6. Phase 0's fixtures do not exist in the workspace, and payout capture is undefined.** The plan's "supplied-file contract" (719-ID Classic salaries, contest 193028206 entries, 126-row Showdown salaries) references files that are not in the repo; the folder contains `plan.md` and nothing else. The plan correctly notes the entry file lacks field size and payout schedule, then never defines how those arrive: there is no operator procedure for capturing a contest's payout table, so the hard gate for payout-aware selection blocks forever by default. Fix in D6.

**B7. The market-line source, as specified, does not exist.** "Timestamped nflverse schedule record" overstates the data: the schedule file updates every 5 minutes in season ([nflverse data schedule](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/nflverse_data_schedule.html)), but its spread/total fields carry no per-line timestamp, no book attribution, and no update guarantee, so the plan's own "never silently reuse a stale line" gate cannot be evaluated against it. The stale-line check would pass on stale lines. Fix in D1 (lines item).

## 3. Structural Vulnerabilities & Over-Engineering Flags

**V1. Governance absorbs half the build.** Twelve public contracts, seven evidence states, a twelve-label monotonic state machine, a separately-pathed referee, and certification manifests are individually defensible and collectively ~40–50% of total effort before any projection edge exists. The referee "separate path" in particular buys little over a same-process independent reparse module with its own parser code, and the evidence taxonomy can collapse (`CONFLICTED` and `STALE` are both `FAIL` with a reason field). Governance should be bought by the unit, not as a lifestyle.

**V2. The possession-level simulator is a research project wearing an implementation's clothes.** Steps 1–6 of the joint simulation amount to building a calibrated generative play-by-play engine: score/time state, play selection, OT, kicking, turnovers. That is months of calibration work, and a miscalibrated game engine produces worse joint distributions than a well-fit correlation model over player outcomes, while being far harder to diagnose. The exact accounting invariants (passing yards equal receiving yards, TDs pair) are the main thing the possession sim buys; they are also the least important property for portfolio EV relative to getting marginal tails and pairwise correlations right. A latent-factor copula fit on a decade of nflverse joint outcomes gets 90% of the value for 10% of the effort and is directly testable against historical correlation matrices.

**V3. The ownership model's cold-start calibration is circular.** A partial-pooling constrained choice model plus a contest-archetype mixture, calibrated on seven structural targets, presumes data that does not exist until slates settle: before week 1 there is nothing to calibrate the mixture against, and the standings exports it will later learn from cover only contests the operator entered (a biased sample of contest types and stakes). The plan's honesty about this ("stress-tested priors, not calibrated ownership claims") is not matched by a correspondingly humble first version. The cold-start model should be a transparent heuristic (salary/projection/value rank softmax with archetype templates), because a wrong simple model can be reasoned about on a Sunday morning and a wrong hierarchical one cannot.

**V4. Half the Pass-1 QA criteria are vibes.** "Fragile chalk," "weak leverage," and "conflicting game scripts within a lineup" have no registered numeric definitions, and an adversarial loop without numeric triggers degenerates into arbitrary swaps, the precise failure the three-pass structure exists to prevent. The repair whitelist and the REFEREE firewall are strong; the plan also never states what happens when REFEREE disagrees with SELECT (re-select? block? log?), leaving the system's last verification step without semantics. Separately, "adjust a constraint supported by measured SELECT improvement" across three passes is a garden of forking paths: repeated adaptive queries against one bank will find noise, and only a pre-registered minimum effect size under the common-random-numbers pairing defends against it.

**V5. The learning loop's automation is dead weight at NFL sample sizes.** By the plan's own thresholds, realized ROI gains authority only after 200 settled slates: 5–6 seasons at roughly two slates a week. Meanwhile "roll back automatically if live grading breaches registered tolerances" will trigger on noise with ~20 observations per season, so the champion/challenger registry, drift monitors, and rollback machinery are being built for decisions that statistics forbids for years. The as-of-correct prospective ledger is the valuable part; the deployment automation around it is not.

**V6. DESIGN is too small to discover the shapes the candidate step exists to find.** Two thousand joint slate scenarios give each game 2,000 draws; a 1-in-500 game shape (the backup-TE-three-score script that wins tournaments) appears ~4 times and survives into a scenario-optimal solve approximately never. Candidate families patch this by hand, but the stated selling point is that structures "emerge from scenarios." Either DESIGN grows to ≥10k with stratified tail oversampling (importance weights recorded), or the plan should stop claiming emergence.

**V7. Windows realities the plan ignores.** If `C:\Users\benja\Documents` is OneDrive-synced, SQLite-in-WAL under a sync client is a known corruption path, and background re-hashing of "immutable" artifacts will produce spurious certification invalidations. An open Excel workbook locks the file against the build (versioned generation mitigates; the plan should state it as the reason). Long-path issues with deep run-registry trees are a real risk under the 260-char default. Phase 1 must verify sync status and relocate or exclude the stores accordingly.

**V8. The Phase 2 gate is a limbo bar on the floor.** "Beat declared salary/position and simple historical baselines" is beatable by any competent model while still losing to every projection source the field actually uses, which is what determines leverage. The gate should add at least one external free reference snapshot (operator-pasted weekly consensus is fine) so "beat the baseline" means something about the market.

**V9. Contest selection is out of scope and unpriced.** Reserved entries arrive as given, yet which contests to enter (overlay, payout curve steepness, field size versus edge) moves long-run ROI more than any lineup micro-decision this engine makes. Fine to cut, but the plan should say the EV consequence out loud and keep a manual screening checklist next to the reservation step.

**V10. Era drift has two live examples the modeling section should name.** The 2024 dynamic-kickoff rules shifted return-TD and starting-field-position regimes (DST and returner pricing), and the 2025+ depth-chart schema change breaks naive weekly joins. Rolling-origin training only defends against these if features carry explicit era flags; "distribution-shift checks" as a phrase does not.

## 4. Concrete Remediation Directives

**D1. Rewrite the game-day evidence rows to match verified reality.**
- Injuries/designations: automated daily pull from the free, documented, keyless [Sleeper API](https://docs.sleeper.com/) players endpoint (`injury_status`, respecting their once-per-day guidance) for Wed–Fri practice designations; nflverse injuries row deleted until the feed exists again.
- Game-day actives/inactives: operator paste is the **primary** path, designed as such: a strict paste format (`TEAM, PLAYER, STATUS, SOURCE_URL, HH:MM`), validated against the frozen slate pool with exact DK-name or GSIS-ID match required (fuzzy matches rejected, not guessed), one screen, under two minutes at T-110. Automated sources become corroboration, never the gate.
- Market lines: the-odds-api with the existing key (`spreads,totals,h2h` × `us` = 3 credits/pull; a 6-pull-per-slate-day cadence is ~234 credits/month against the free tier's 500 ([pricing](https://the-odds-api.com/))), capturing per-book `last_update` timestamps into `SourceArtifact` so the stale-line gate is actually evaluable. nflverse schedule lines demote to weekly priors. Operator paste stays as fallback.
- Depth charts: adapter written against the 2025+ timestamped append schema, treated as weekly priors only, never as game-day evidence.

**D2. Objective surgery.** For GPPs: frontier axes become E[net payout] (dup- and tie-adjusted, evaluated in-scenario) and P(elite finish), where elite = 99.9th percentile for fields ≥ 50k and 99th below; NonWashout becomes a chance constraint, P(gross < fees) ≤ β set by bankroll policy, not a maximized axis. Keep Nash-product selection on the new axes if desired. Cash contests keep the plan's spec (P(above the line), EV tiebreak), which is already correct. For n ≤ 3 entries, skip the frontier entirely: exhaustive or ranked selection on contest EV with a pairwise-correlation penalty. Any portfolio whose simulated E[net] < −X% of fees is rejected regardless of frontier position, with X registered per contest type.

**D3. Insulate selection from field-model error instead of building on top of it.** Selection evaluates every candidate portfolio under all three ownership-reaction states and optimizes the worst-state value (or a registered weighting); while the `COLD_START_FIELD_MODEL` grade is active, leverage components 2–4 (tail-optimality vs field, duplication edge, marginal scenario coverage vs field) are shrunk toward zero and the portfolio leans on component 1 plus correlation quality. Field-model influence ramps up only as post-slate calibration reports clear registered thresholds. This turns "honest labeling" into an actual control.

**D4. Publish compute budgets and make them phase gates.** Mandates: field scoring reduces to per-scenario order statistics (payout-tier threshold vectors plus a canonical-lineup dup hash), memory O(scenarios × payout tiers), never a field × scenario matrix (5.6–8.9 GiB at real sizes); candidate × scenario matrices stored f16/chunked with a live-candidate cap (~20–50k); the simulator vectorizes across scenarios from day one (16.8M plays per Classic SELECT bank is minutes-to-half-hours scalar, sub-2-minutes vectorized); MILP via `highspy` directly (warm starts, incumbent retention) rather than the SciPy wrapper. Gates with numbers, measured on the actual operator machine and recorded in the run manifest: full Classic rebuild ≤ 10 min, late-swap cycle ≤ 5 min, certify ≤ 2 min.

**D5. Define the degraded modes and re-sequence for the calendar.** Ship order: (a) by Week 1, Phase 0–1 only, used as a guardrail: hand-built lineups pass through legality, template, and byte certification; (b) by ~Week 3, a Classic Manual-Mode MVP: nflreadpy snapshots, baseline opportunity projections with uncertainty, a latent-factor copula simulator (V2) instead of the possession engine, MILP candidates, D2 objectives with paste-based evidence, 2-entry exhaustive selection. The copula ships with registered tail and dependence acceptance targets fit from 2019–2025 nflverse history, not hardcoded: per-position, per-salary-tier exceedance rates at 2x/3x/4x of salary-implied median within ±25% relative of empirical, and QB-to-pass-catcher pairwise correlations inside the fitted historical bands. Miss the targets and the bank is not used for selection; (c) Weeks 4–8, heuristic ownership, order-statistics field evaluation, full portfolio modes, late swap; (d) midseason, mixture-model field calibration from accumulated standings and the learning ledger; possession simulation only if the copula's correlation calibration fails its targets. Showdown remains blocked exactly as the plan says until an entry template and payout capture exist, then reuses the stack.

**D6. Fixture and payout intake, this week.** Commit the four DK CSVs to `fixtures/` with hashes recorded before anything else is written; add the missing operator procedure for contest economics: copy the payout table from the DK contest page into `payouts_<contest_id>.csv` (rank_from, rank_to, prize), validated for monotonicity, full rank coverage, and a total matching the advertised prize pool, hash-frozen into the `ContestContract`. Without this file the contest stays in the plan's own `DO_NOT_UPLOAD` path, which is correct; now it is also exitable.

**D7. Replace learning automation with versioned refits.** Weekly refit produces a challenger artifact plus a diagnostic report (proper scoring, calibration, drift, as before); promotion is a one-command manual step; rollback is re-pointing to the prior artifact; shrinkage follows a fixed registered schedule rather than adaptive tolerances. The prospective ledger, as-of snapshots, and immutable model history stay exactly as designed; they are the part with real value at n ≈ 36 slates/season.

**D8. Give the QA loop numbers and REFEREE semantics.** Every Pass-1 category gets a registered numeric trigger (examples to register: portfolio exposure to any single game > G%; any lineup with modeled p95 duplication > D for the field size; chalk fragility = ownership > A% ∧ unresolved status; salary-left outside the field model's p5–p95 without a scenario-coverage justification). A repair is accepted only if paired-CRN SELECT improvement ≥ a pre-registered δ, once, no re-tries against the same finding. REFEREE is report-only and binding in one direction: a sign disagreement with SELECT on E[net] or the washout constraint is a blocker; magnitude gaps inside the noise band are logged. If any qualitative/LLM reviewer participates in QA or evidence entry, every claim it records must carry source URL, quoted text, and capture timestamp, re-fetch-verified before it can flip any evidence state; an unverifiable claim records as `UNKNOWN` and cannot gate.

**D9. Certify early, improve optionally.** The cadence's single T-30 certify-and-upload is a self-inflicted race against the invalidation cascade: any T-20 scratch leaves reserved entries carrying whatever was last uploaded. Policy: a legal, certified package uploads at T-60 unconditionally; later re-certifications (news, QA repairs) are strict upgrades uploaded only if complete before T-10; the panic path ("upload the last certified package, stop") is documented on the workbook's first sheet.

**D10. Operator surface trims.** One `run` meta-command that walks intake → certify with explicit stop points, wrapping the eleven subcommands (which remain for surgical use); workbook starts at five sheets (Run Control, Evidence Paste, Portfolio, QA, Upload) and earns the other seven back as the phases that populate them ship; Phase 1 includes the OneDrive/sync check with store relocation if positive (V7), plus a long-path and Excel-lock test on the real machine.

## What survives unchanged

The DK file boundary and fair-play posture, the immutable snapshot/hash/evidence architecture, scenario-native candidate generation, roster-accounting ownership with the lineup-probability ban, the DESIGN/SELECT/REFEREE separation, exact tie/dup payout math, `NOT_YET_DUE` semantics with mandatory late swap, Showdown single-lock handling, the acceptance-matrix habit, and the refusal to certify EV. Build those as written.
